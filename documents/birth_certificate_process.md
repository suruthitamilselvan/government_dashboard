# Birth Certificate — Application Process and Guidelines

**Issuing Authority:** Municipal Corporation / Gram Panchayat / Notified Area Authority  
**Governed By:** Registration of Births and Deaths Act, 1969  
**Source:** Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India

---

## 1. Overview

A birth certificate is a vital document issued by the government that legally records the birth of a child. It is mandatory for school admissions, passports, voter ID, Aadhaar registration, marriage registration, and inheritance claims. In India, every birth must be registered within **21 days** of occurrence.

---

## 2. Who Can Apply

- Parent or guardian of the child
- Head of the household where the birth occurred
- Medical officer in charge of the hospital where birth occurred
- Local registrar on suo motu basis in certain cases

---

## 3. Where to Apply

| Birth Location | Registration Authority |
|---|---|
| Hospital / Nursing Home | Hospital medical officer submits report; parent collects certificate from Municipal Office |
| Home (Urban) | Ward office / Municipal Corporation |
| Home (Rural) | Gram Panchayat / Block Development Office |
| Online | State government's Civil Registration System (CRS) portal |

---

## 4. Documents Required

### Mandatory Documents
1. **Hospital Discharge Summary** (if born in hospital) OR **Declaration of birth** from head of family (if home birth)
2. **Proof of parents' identity** — Aadhaar card, Voter ID, or Passport (any one)
3. **Proof of parents' address** — Aadhaar, ration card, electricity bill, or rental agreement (any one)
4. **Marriage certificate of parents** (recommended; may be mandatory in some states)
5. **Informant's signature / thumb impression** on the prescribed form

### Additional Documents (if applicable)
- Affidavit on stamp paper if registration is delayed beyond 21 days
- Court order if delayed beyond 1 year

---

## 5. Application Process

### Step 1 — Fill the Prescribed Form
Obtain Form No. 1 (Birth Report Form) from the local registrar's office or download from the state CRS portal.

### Step 2 — Submit the Application
Submit the completed form along with all required documents at:
- Municipal Corporation ward office, OR
- Gram Panchayat office, OR
- Online via the state CRS portal (e.g., crsorgi.gov.in)

### Step 3 — Verification
The registrar verifies the documents. If born in hospital, the birth report submitted by the hospital is cross-referenced.

### Step 4 — Registration Entry
The birth is entered into the Birth Register. A unique **Registration Number** is assigned.

### Step 5 — Certificate Issuance
- **Within 21 days of birth:** Free of charge; certificate issued within 7 working days.
- **21 days to 30 days:** Application to sub-registrar; nominal fee (₹2–₹10 depending on state).
- **30 days to 1 year:** Application to revenue officer with late fee (typically ₹25–₹50).
- **After 1 year:** Magistrate order required; affidavit and late fee mandatory.

---

## 6. Online Application (CRS Portal — crsorgi.gov.in)

1. Register on the CRS portal.
2. Select your state and local body.
3. Fill the online birth report form.
4. Upload scanned documents.
5. Submit and note the acknowledgement number.
6. Download the digitally signed certificate once approved (typically 3–7 days).

---

## 7. Fees

| Scenario | Fee |
|---|---|
| Within 21 days | Free |
| 21–30 days | ₹2–₹10 (state-specific) |
| 30 days to 1 year | ₹25–₹50 + affidavit |
| After 1 year | ₹50–₹100 + court order |
| Duplicate certificate | ₹20–₹50 |

## 8. Common Rejection Reasons for Birth Certificate Applications

Applications are frequently rejected for the following reasons. Understanding these helps you avoid delays:

1. **Incomplete form:** Fields such as child's exact time of birth, place of birth, or parent details left blank. Ensure every field is filled accurately.
2. **Mismatch of names across documents:** Parent name on Aadhaar differs from name on hospital records. Submit a self-declaration affidavit explaining the discrepancy.
3. **Unattested documents:** Photocopies not self-attested or attested by a gazetted officer where required.
4. **Delayed registration without affidavit:** Applications submitted after 30 days without an accompanying affidavit on non-judicial stamp paper are summarily rejected.
5. **Wrong jurisdiction:** Application submitted to a ward office outside the area where the birth occurred. Verify the correct local body boundary.
6. **Missing hospital report:** For institutional births, the hospital's birth report (Form 1 submitted by medical officer) must be on record. If the hospital has not submitted this, follow up with the hospital first.
7. **Signature mismatch:** Informant's signature in the form differs from the ID document. Use a consistent signature.
8. **Duplicate application:** A birth certificate already exists in the register for the same child. Reapplication will be rejected; request a duplicate certificate instead.
9. **Non-payment of late fee:** Applications after 21 days submitted without the prescribed late fee challan.

### How to Appeal a Rejection
- Obtain the **rejection letter** citing specific reasons.
- Rectify the stated deficiency.
- Re-submit with a covering letter referencing the original application number.
- If the rejection is disputed, appeal to the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM) within **30 days** of the rejection order.

---

## 9. Corrections in Birth Certificate

If there is an error in the issued certificate (e.g., misspelled name, wrong date):
1. Apply to the registrar with proof documents showing the correct information.
2. A correction fee (₹50–₹200, state-specific) applies.
3. Corrections after 1 year require a magistrate's order.

## 10. Helpline and Grievance Redressal

- **National Helpline:** 1800-110-001 (toll-free, Ministry of Home Affairs)
- **State-specific portals:** Visit your state government's civil registration page.
- **Online grievance:** pgportal.gov.in
- Response time for grievances: 30 working days as per Citizens' Charter.
